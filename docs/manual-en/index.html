Spektrafilm Darkroom

A manual for photographers who know Capture One and want to develop photos as if they went onto film and then onto paper in a darkroom. It describes every function of the panel and guides you from preparing a photo to export.

Where to start

If you are working with Spektrafilm for the first time, read the first three chapters in order. They take about half an hour and save you a lot of trial and error.

  • How to think about Spektrafilm explains why brightness and contrast are set differently here than with the C1 sliders.
  • Quick start takes you through your first photo in ten steps.
  • Preparing in Capture One tells you which C1 adjustments to keep and which to leave to the film.

The panel works only when Capture One is started through the Spektrafilm shortcut; chapter 16 describes this. The cheat sheet collects the most important controls.

When you want a specific effect, go straight to the recipes. When something looks wrong, start with the Troubleshooting and limitations chapter.

Chapters

The order of the chapters follows the order of work: from the stock and exposure, through colour, optics and grain, to finishing and export. The strip at the top of each page works like a test strip from the darkroom: each step is a chapter.

    How to read the function descriptions

    Every control in the panel is described the same way: what it does, its range and starting value, when to use it, typical values, what it works with and what to watch out for. Control names are in English, exactly as in the panel, for example Print exposure.

    In the darkroom

    These boxes show the equivalent of a function in a traditional darkroom. They help you understand why a control works the way it does.

    Tip

    Yellow boxes are practical advice from work on real photos.

    Pitfall

    Pink boxes warn about settings that are easy to get wrong or that work differently from what the name suggests.

    The values in the recipes are starting points tested on real photos. On another photo you will fine-tune them by eye, but the direction of the changes will be the same.

    What this manual does not promise

    Spektrafilm simulates the physical process from the technical data of films and papers. The result is credible, but it is not a copy of a specific print from a specific lab. The descriptions of film character separate what the manufacturer claims from what you see in the simulation. Experimental functions are clearly marked.
