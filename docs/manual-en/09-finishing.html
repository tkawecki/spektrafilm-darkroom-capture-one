Finishing

After the print comes the scan: the scanner has its own white, black, sharpness and glare, and after that you can still finish the image with curves, levels and a crop. This chapter covers everything that happens after the paper simulation.

Order in the pipeline

  1. Enlarger positioning: enlarges part of the negative, before the print.
  2. Scanning: scan white and black, glare, scanner sharpness.
  3. Post-scan finishing: exposure, contrast, curves, levels, saturation, toning.
  4. Scan geometry: crop, rotation and desqueeze, after the C1 crop.
  5. Paper textures and border: paper texture and burnt-in border, at the very end.

Sharpness

With the default settings the print is softer than the same image in Capture One. The simulation does not blur the photo: with Defaults, halation, scatter, diffusion filters, grain and the scanner are all off. The softness comes from the input. The Neutral for film preparation feeds the simulation an image without C1 sharpening, without Clarity and Structure (also on layers) and without output sharpening (chapter 3).

In the darkroom

Film and prints have no digital sharpening. The sharpness of film is the sharpness of the lens, the edge effect from development and, possibly, sharpening in the scanner. A sharpened input image would also give halos around the halation and harder grain.

You restore sharpness with tools that have a counterpart in the film process, in this order:

  1. Scanner sharpening. The Scanning section is inside Spektrafilm optics, below the grain sections; do not confuse it with Scan geometry in Post-scan finishing. In Spektrafilm optics › Scanning, turn on Enable scanner controls. Set Scanner MTF50 (lp/mm) to 0, which turns off the blur of the scanner optics, and Scanner glare to 0 if you do not want glare. Then choose Unsharp amount: 0.7 gives sharpness close to the image in C1, 1–1.5 clearly more. Unsharp radius (µm) 5 corresponds to about 1 pixel at 40–50 MP.
  2. Micro contrast. Micro contrast in Post-scan finishing, +0.1 to +0.3. It works like Clarity: it brings out texture, not edges. On its own it does not replace sharpening: +0.2 lifts fine detail only moderately.
  3. Film edge effect. Diffusion width (µm) in Coupler diffusion, 5–20 µm. It gives the edge sharpness typical of film (chapter 6).
Pitfall: turning on the scanner alone does not sharpen

Enable scanner controls applies the default values at once: optics blur (MTF50 60 lp/mm, about 0.5–0.7 pixel at 24–50 MP), sharpening 0.7 and, in Print Simulation, glare 0.03. Together these weaken the finest detail and slightly strengthen medium detail. So for clean sharpening, set MTF50 to 0.

  • Judge sharpness and grain at 100% zoom with Viewer resolution = Auto or Native resolution; in Auto, when you view the whole photo, the frame is the size of the screen. At 400% the Spektrafilm preview is also smoothed by the scaling.
  • Scanner sharpening also emphasises grain. Set the grain first, then the sharpness.
  • An export with neutral input does not get the C1 output sharpening from the recipe. Set sharpness in the panel or later in another program.
  • Switching the input to Capture One development brings back C1 sharpening, but together with the C1 curve, so with double contrast. We do not recommend it.

Fine detail is the contrast of details up to about 2 pixels, medium detail 2–6 pixels; both are compared with the coarser structure of the same image, so that a difference in the tone curve does not distort the result. Scanning

Enable scanner controls

Spektrafilm optics › Scanning

Default
off

Turns on all the scanner settings below. When off, they keep their saved values. Once you turn it on, the default values apply at once: optics blur (MTF50 60 lp/mm), sharpening 0.7 and, in Print Simulation, glare 0.03.

Correct white level and White point

Spektrafilm optics › Scanning

White point
0–1, default 0.98
Correct white level
off by default

Sets the level (in sRGB encoding) given to the brightest print this film can produce on this paper. Middle grey stays in place, because the panel recalculates the print exposure, so mainly the highlights move. In an inverted negative scan it applies to the brightest density of the film.

The brightest whites rise a little. It needs Enable scanner controls and works in Print Simulation and in an inverted negative scan.

When to use

When the print looks “dirty” in the whites and you want clean, screen-like highlights, for example in architecture.

Correct black level and Black point

Spektrafilm optics › Scanning

Black point
0–1, default 0.01 (about 3/255)
Correct black level
off by default

The same for the deepest black of the print. The default 0.01 brings the paper black (about 14/255) down to about 3/255.

Pitfalls

A full black takes away the character of the paper. Try the white correction on its own first.

Scanner glare, Glare roughness, Glare blur (px)

Spektrafilm optics › Scanning, Print Simulation only

Scanner glare
0–0.2, default 0.03
Glare roughness
0–4, default 0.7
Glare blur
0–32 px, default 0.5

Stray light in the scanner or when viewing the print. It lifts the black and lowers shadow contrast, with a slight random unevenness. At 0.1 the black rises only slightly.

Pitfalls

Glare blur is measured in output pixels, so on a small preview it looks wider than on a full export. The glare is random, so it is not included in a LUT export.

Scanner MTF50, Unsharp radius, Unsharp amount

Spektrafilm optics › Scanning

Scanner MTF50 (lp/mm)
0–300, default 60
Unsharp radius (µm)
0–100, default 5
Unsharp amount
0–4, default 0.7

The sharpness of the scanner optics (resolution at 50% contrast) and unsharp-mask sharpening, as in scanner software. MTF50 of 0 turns off the optics blur; the higher the value, the less blur. The default 60 lp/mm is a blur with a radius of about 3 µm on the film. The sharpening radius is in micrometres on the film, so it scales with Film format.

When to use

A lower MTF (but above zero) gives a soft, “home” scan. A higher Unsharp amount gives a sharp lab scan with more visible grain. How to restore sharpness after neutral input is described in the Sharpness section.

Enlarger positioning

Enlarger scale, Horizontal offset (%), Vertical offset (%)

Spektrafilm optics › Enlarger positioning

Enlarger scale
1–32, default 1
Offsets (%)
−100 to 100, default 0

Enlarges part of the negative, like raising the enlarger head. 1 is the full frame. Higher values crop and enlarge, so grain, halation and blurs grow with the image. The offsets choose which part.

Post-scan finishing

Finishing works after the print simulation and before Look strength. It has the same tools as an ordinary editor, but applied to the finished print. Use them sparingly: most of the look should come from the materials.

Enable post-scan finishing

Post-scan finishing

Default
on (changes nothing at neutral values)

Turns all finishing on or off. Useful for a quick comparison with the print without finishing.

Finishing exposure (EV)

Post-scan finishing

Range
−4 to 4 EV
Default
0

The brightness of the finished scan, like the exposure slider in an editor. +0.5 EV brightens the midtones and lifts the whites.

Pitfalls

This is not the exposure of the film or the paper: it does not change the character of the curves. Use Film exposure and Print exposure first.

Contrast of the finished image. Very strong: ±0.5 is an extreme effect. Typically ±0.05–0.2.

Post-scan finishing

Local contrast of fine detail, like Clarity. It has a fixed radius in output pixels, so its visible strength depends on the resolution. Judge it in the preview at 100% or on the export. It is not included in a LUT export.

Saturation changes the saturation of all colours equally. Vibrance lifts weakly saturated colours more and spares colours that are already saturated, such as skin.

Hue saturation

Six saturation sliders for colour ranges: Red saturation, Yellow saturation, Green saturation, Cyan saturation, Blue saturation and Magenta saturation. Range 0–2, default 1. Example: Blue saturation 0.5 calms the sky, and the other colours stay.

Point curves

Four curves: Master curve and the Red curve, Green curve and Blue curve. Each has five points with a fixed input (0, 25, 50, 75 and 100%), and the sliders set their output: Black output, Shadows output, Midtones output, Highlights output and White output (0–1; default 0 / 0.25 / 0.5 / 0.75 / 1).

Edit curve…, Use five-point controls

Post-scan finishing › Point curves › each curve

Edit curve… opens an editor where you place the points freely, with the mouse or by typing percentages (both a point and a comma work as the decimal separator). The curve then becomes a custom curve, and the five-point sliders are inactive. Use five-point controls returns to the saved sliders.

Scan geometry

Scan geometry works after finishing and after the C1 crop. The Viewer fits the result to the photo area in C1, and the export keeps the dimensions of the result. A LUT export ignores the geometry.

Pitfall

Rotation without a crop leaves white triangles in the corners, like the scanner background. When you rotate, turn on Enable scan crop and make the crop smaller, or rotate the photo in C1.

Paper textures and border

Edit paper layers…

Paper textures and border

Opens the paper texture editor: up to 32 texture layers from your own files (paper scans, textures), with blend mode and opacity, plus a burnt-in border as a fraction of the long side. Texture and border work at the very end, after scan geometry. Active layers limit the finished image to the range of the paper, also in linear DNG. Show source skips them.

Textures are copied into the panel’s store (the Paper textures folder next to the panel log), so the look still works after you move the original file. A texture file can be up to 64 MB, 16 MP and 8192 px per side, and the border width is given in percent (0–100%).

The border makes the output file larger: a margin as wide as the given fraction of the long side is added on each side, filled with paper white and the textures.

Pitfalls

The burnt-in border works only together with at least one visible texture layer, as in the reference implementation. A border alone without a texture does not change the image; the summary below the button then says that the finishing has no effect.
