Films and papers

Choosing the film and the paper is the most important decision in Spektrafilm. The film decides how the scene is recorded on the negative. The paper decides how the negative becomes a print: its contrast, its black and its saturation.

How to choose

Start with the pairing the profile author intended, and change one element at a time:

FilmsDefault partner
Kodak still films (Portra, Ektar, Gold, Ultramax)Kodak Professional Portra Endura
Fujifilm still films (C200, Pro 400H, X-Tra 400)Fujifilm Crystal Archive Type II
Motion picture films (Vision2, Vision3, Verita, Eterna)Kodak Vision 2383
Slides (Provia, Velvia, Ektachrome, Kodachrome)No paper, Scan mode

The panel does not change the paper by itself when you change the film. You choose it deliberately in Print stock.

Tip

The film mainly changes colour and saturation. The paper mainly changes contrast and the depth of the black. When the image is too flat or too hard, change the paper. When the colours are too weak or too strong, change the film.

Controls

Processing mode

Top of the panel

Options
Print Simulation, Scan Film
Default
Print Simulation

Chooses the process. Print Simulation prints the negative on paper. Scan Film shows the film alone, as a scanner sees it: a negative or a slide.

When to use

Print Simulation for negative films. Scan for slides, and when you want the look of a negative scan without paper.

Pitfalls

In Scan mode Print stock, Print exposure and the whole Enlarger and print section are greyed out. Their values are kept and come back when you switch to Print.

Film stock

Top of the panel

Default
Kodak Portra 400
Profiles
21 negatives, plus 5 slides in Scan mode

The film you “shoot” on. The profile holds the spectral sensitivity of the layers, the characteristic curves, the DIR couplers, the graininess and the susceptibility to halation.

Pitfalls

Slides appear in the list only in Scan mode. Tungsten (T) films (200T, 500T, 100T) do not turn the photo blue, because the simulation balances the image to the film's white. You get the “tungsten film in daylight” effect with white balance in C1 or with filtration.

Print stock

Top of the panel, Print Simulation only

Default
Kodak Professional Portra Endura
Profiles
6 papers, 2 motion picture print films

The print material. It decides the contrast, the depth of the black, the saturation and how the negative's dyes print.

Invert negative scan

Below the film list, only in Scan mode with a negative

Default
off

Turns the scanned negative into a linear positive. This image has no paper character and is usually too bright, so you have to balance it with Film exposure or in the Scanning section. For normal work Print Simulation is the better choice.

Motion picture films

Motion picture negatives (Vision, Verita, Eterna) have low saturation and a soft gradation by design, because they were made for further processing. In the simulation on Portra Endura they come out warmer, slightly less contrasty and less saturated than Portra 400. They show their full character only on the 2383 or 2393 print film.

According to Kodak, Vision3 has about 2 EV of extra latitude in the highlights, less grain in the shadows and good skin. Verita 200D (2026) has a deliberately shorter latitude than Vision3. The Vision2, Eterna 250D, Ultramax 800 and Velvia 100F profiles were added later; their source is listed in Sources and licences. They are derived from neighbouring profiles and the manufacturer's data sheets, not measured from scratch.

Pitfall

Choosing a T film does not give a blue photo from daylight. In the simulation T films even come out warmer than daylight films (Vision3 500T is warmer than Vision3 250D). Create a cold, night mood with white balance in C1 or with enlarger filtration.

In the darkroom

Photographic paper has a limited black. On Portra Endura the darkest parts of the print stay above screen black in the simulation (Ultra Endura goes deeper), and the brightest parts usually stay below screen white, because the paper's white is not the pure white of a screen. The 2383 and 2393 print films go down to full black, which gives a deep, cinematic shadow. You can stretch the white and the black in the Scanning section (chapter 9).

Slides

A slide is reversal film: after development it is already a positive. You view it on a light box or scan it, without paper. In the panel set Processing mode = Scan Film, and the slides appear in the film list.

In the simulation Velvia and Provia have more contrast than a negative on Portra Endura, Ektachrome a little more, and Kodachrome the same. All slides go down to a deep black and are warmer, Kodachrome the most. Velvia 100 is the most saturated, slightly more than Portra 400 on Endura; Provia, Ektachrome and Kodachrome are less saturated than that negative. A slide has a narrow latitude, so you have to set the exposure (Film exposure) more carefully than with a negative: the highlights easily lose detail.

Negative scan

In Scan mode with a negative film you see the negative itself, with its orange mask. The Invert negative scan option inverts it into a linear positive without paper character.

How to compare materials

  1. Set up a look with one pairing and click Pin reference. The panel remembers the current image.
  2. Change the film or the paper.
  3. Click Compare reference…. A window opens with the modes Side by side, Vertical wipe (with a split slider), Reference only and Current only. The window follows the current Viewer, so you can keep changing settings. Clear reference removes the remembered image.
  4. When you want to compare for longer, make several clone variants in C1 with different pairings.
