Recipes

Ready-made set-ups for travel, architecture and portraits. Each one starts from Defaults (Portra 400 on Portra Endura, neutral input, automatic metering) and lists only what you change. The examples with the village photo start instead from the look its author made for it (Kodak Gold 200 on Portra Endura, film exposure +1.4 EV); there a recipe's film exposure is a change of that value. The values were checked by rendering example photos. On your own photo you fine-tune them by eye, but the direction stays the same.

How to use them

Set up a recipe, save it as a preset (Save preset…), and on the next photos adjust mainly Film exposure and Print exposure. Drag the slider on a comparison to see the image before and after.

Natural landscape

Goal: believable travel colours, gentle highlights, a calm sky and calm water.

Film, paperKodak Portra 400, Kodak Professional Portra Endura
Film exposure−0.3 EV
Print highlight shape−0.3
Grain modelProduction

What to watch: clouds and other bright areas should keep detail, and greens should not look garish. If the photo looks too pale, switch the paper to Supra Endura.

DIR coupler amount1.2
Grain modelProduction

Variants: even stronger on Ultra Endura or Endura Premier. The recipe clearly raises saturation, and most of that change comes from DIR coupler amount 1.2.

Harsh sun and rescuing highlights

Goal: a scene with hard sunlight and deep shadow, where the highlights burn out and the print is too hard.

  1. Film exposure −0.5 EV.
  2. Print gamma 0.85.
  3. Preflash exposure 0.01 and Print exposure −0.2 EV.
  4. If the highlights still look flat: Print highlight shape −0.3 to −0.5.

What to watch: the brightest parts of the frame, such as the sky. The print becomes darker and calmer; set the brightness last with Print exposure. Each step is explained in chapter 5.

Architecture and white walls

Goal: clean whites that keep detail, neutral concrete, moderate contrast.

Film, paperKodak Portra 160, Kodak Professional Portra Endura
Film exposure−0.3 EV
Print highlight shape−0.5
Enable scanner controls and Correct white levelon
Grain modelProduction

What to watch: light walls and stone show clearly more tonal separation than with the default settings. If the white has a colour cast, correct it with Yellow filter shift or Magenta filter shift by 2–5 CC. A variant with stronger colour: Endura Premier (more saturation and contrast).

Backlit portrait, natural skin

Goal: warm, smooth skin, soft highlights in the hair, a gentle glow.

The example uses the village scene: judge its white walls and warm roof tiles the way you would judge skin tones.

Film, paperKodak Portra 400, Kodak Professional Portra Endura
Film exposure−0.3 EV
Print gamma0.9
Print highlight shape−0.4
Halation amount, Highlight boost (EV)1, 3
Camera diffusionBlack Pro-Mist, Filter strength 0.3 (type it into the field; the slider moves in steps of 0.125)
Grain modelProduction

What to watch: skin on the cheeks and forehead. The darkroom rule: add a filter in the colour of the cast. Skin too pink: 2–5 CC magenta. Too yellow: 2–5 CC yellow. Too red: 2–3 CC each of magenta and yellow.

Cinematic look

Goal: deep black, a warm tone, glowing highlights, visible grain.

Film, paperKodak Vision3 250D (daylight) or Vision3 500T (evening), Kodak Vision 2383
Halation amount, Highlight boost (EV)1–2, 3–6
Camera diffusionBlack Pro-Mist, Filter strength 0.25–0.5
Grain modelProduction; for stronger grain, Film format Super 35 or 16 mm
Pitfall

Vision 2383 goes down to full black: in a dark scene a large part of the frame can turn black. If the shadows merge, raise Film exposure or set Print shadow shape to a positive value.

Dusk and low light

Goal: keep the mood of a dark scene instead of brightening it into grey.

Film, paperKodak Portra 800, Kodak Professional Portra Endura
Film exposure−0.5 EV
Print shadow shape−0.3
Grain modelProduction

Here Film exposure sets the mood: −0.5 EV darkens the whole image and keeps its evening character. When centre-weighted metering reads a bright sky, turning metering off gives a brighter print, not a darker one. A variant with more contrast: Portra 800 (Push 1) or (Push 2), each a little harder.

Slide

Goal: strong, clean colours and the hard contrast of slide film, without paper.

  1. Processing mode = Scan Film.
  2. Film stock: Fujifilm Velvia 100 (the most saturated) or Provia 100F (calmer).
  3. Film exposure −0.3 EV: slide film has a short tonal range, so highlights easily lose detail.
  4. Grain model: Production.

Black-and-white print from colour film

The panel has no black-and-white films. You make a monochrome image in scan finishing: it is a black-and-white copy of a colour print, not a simulation of black-and-white film.

  1. Set up the look in colour: film, paper, exposure and contrast. They decide how the tones are distributed.
  2. In Post-scan finishing, set Saturation to 0.
  3. Refine the contrast with Print gamma or the Master curve.
  4. Add a print tone in Split toning: Shadow strength and Highlight strength 0.05–0.15, for example warm highlights (45°) and cool shadows (210°).
  5. Grain: Production with Grain saturation 0.

Enlarger filtration before desaturating works only roughly like colour filters in black-and-white photography.

Measurement: Portra 400 on Portra Endura. Saturation 0 gives an exactly neutral image (R = G = B). Print gamma 1.2 darkens the midtones, lifts the highlights and deepens the black slightly. Split toning 0.1 / 0.1 with the default hues (highlights 45°, shadows 210°) makes the highlights slightly warm and the shadows slightly blue.

Artificial light and night

Goal: evening streets, interiors under tungsten bulbs, neon.

Film, paperKodak Vision3 500T, Kodak Vision 2383; calmer: Portra 800 on Portra Endura
White balance in C1A T film does not cool the image by itself. Keep the warmth of the bulbs or correct it with white balance in C1; add a cool "night" tone with Yellow filter shift +5 to +10 CC.
Film exposure−0.5 to −1 EV, so that night stays night
Halation amount, Highlight boost (EV)1–2 and 4–6: lamps and neon get a glow
Camera diffusionBlack Pro-Mist or CineBloom, Filter strength 0.25–0.5
Grain modelProduction; remove high-ISO noise in C1 first

Vision 2383 goes down to full black, so keep an eye on the shadows (Print shadow shape positive). The example photos include no night scene: these values are a starting point put together from measurements on other frames, and they have no illustration.

Grain for print and for screen

These are starting points from practice, not measurements.

  • Screen and web: Production, Grain amount 1–1.5. Downsizing weakens the grain anyway.
  • Large print: Production, amount 0.8–1. Judge it at 100% zoom (Viewer resolution = Auto or Native resolution).
  • Strong cinema grain: Film format 16 mm or Super 16.

A comparison of the models and 100% crops are in chapter 8.

A consistent series from a trip

  1. Pick an "average" photo from the series: typical light, no extremes.
  2. Set up the look on it and save it as a preset.
  3. Copy look, select the whole series, Apply copied look to selected.
  4. Go through the photos. Automatic metering evens out the brightness, so you usually only correct Film exposure on photos taken into the sun or in shade.
  5. To compare, use Pin reference on the reference photo and Compare reference… on the others.
  6. Batch export: Export selected TIFFs… or a normal C1 export with one recipe.

Your own library of looks

Save every successful look as a preset with a name that describes the materials and the use, for example "Portra 400 Endura soft backlight.spektra.json". Keep the presets in one folder. On a new photo: Load preset…, then only the exposure. The photo's previous look is saved as a version.
