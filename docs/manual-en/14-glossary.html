Glossary

Darkroom, film and colour terms that appear in the panel and in this manual.

Materials and process

NegativeFilm on which the bright parts of the scene are dark and the colours are inverted. It gives a print only on paper. It has a wide tonal range.
Slide (transparency)Reversal film: after development it is a positive. Narrow latitude, strong contrast and colour. In the panel, only in Scan mode.
RA-4 paperColour photographic paper for prints from negatives (Endura, Crystal Archive, Ektacolor).
Print filmPositive cinema film (Kodak 2383, 2393) onto which the camera negative is printed for projection. Deep black, strong colour.
C-41, ECN-2, E-6, K-14Development processes: photographic negative, cinema negative, slide, Kodachrome.
Rem-jetBlack anti-halation layer on the back of cinema films. Without it (as in CineStill) halation is very strong.
Orange maskThe orange tint of a negative, from coloured couplers that correct imperfections of the dyes. The paper is matched to it.
Base (D-min)The density of unexposed, developed material: the mask of the negative or the tint of the paper.
D-maxThe highest density of a material: the deepest black of the print or the densest area of the negative.

Tonality

DensityA logarithmic measure of how much light a material holds back. Density 1 lets through 10% of the light, 2 lets through 1%.
Characteristic curveDensity as a function of exposure. It has a toe (shadows), a straight section (midtones) and a shoulder (highlights).
GammaThe slope of the straight section of the curve, that is, the contrast in the midtones.
Toe, shoulderThe gentle ends of the curve: the toe at the lowest exposure, the shoulder at the highest.
LatitudeThe range of exposures that a material records with detail.
EVAn exposure step: +1 EV is twice as much light.
DetailVisible tonal differences in an area, for example the texture of clouds in the highlights.
18% greyThe mid tone that a light meter and the panel’s automatic metering aim for.

Darkroom

Print exposureThe time the enlarger light falls on the paper. Longer means darker.
Filtration, CCColour filters in the enlarger head (cyan, magenta, yellow) in CC units. On negative paper the print shifts towards the complementary colour of the added filter: more yellow gives a bluer print, more magenta a greener one, more cyan a redder one.
PreflashA short, even exposure of the paper before the main one, which softens the highlights.
Test stripSeveral exposures of one area at different times, to choose the right one. In the panel: Exposure strips….
Push, pullLonger or shorter development: higher or lower contrast. Push rescues underexposed film, pull softens a scene with too much contrast.
Bleach bypassSkipping the bleach: the silver stays next to the dyes, colour fades, contrast rises.
Exhausted developerDeveloper with a weaker action: heavily exposed areas come out softer and less dense.

Colour and emulsion

Emulsion layersColour film has layers sensitive to blue, green and red; each forms a dye in the complementary colour.
Spectral sensitivityHow strongly a layer responds to each wavelength. It decides how the film “sees” colours.
DIR couplersCompounds that release an inhibitor during development, which holds back development nearby. They give cleaner colours and sharper edges.
HalationA glow around bright highlights from reflection off the film base, usually reddish.
Emulsion scatterScattering of light within the light-sensitive layer itself; a slight softness of edges.
RMS granularityThe grain measure from data sheets: the deviation of density measured with a 48 µm aperture, times 1000.
Spectral reconstructionRebuilding a smooth light spectrum from three RGB values, needed to simulate how the film responds.
GamutThe range of colours that a given space can hold (sRGB, Adobe RGB, ProPhoto…).
Linear ResponseA Capture One development curve without a contrast boost, used as neutral input to the simulation.
D film, T filmFilm balanced for daylight or for tungsten light (about 3200 K).

Scan and export

MTF50The resolution at which the contrast of detail drops to 50%. A measure of scanner sharpness.
Unsharp maskSharpening by subtracting a blurred copy of the image.
GlareStray light in the scanner that lifts the black.
3D LUTA colour conversion table that other programs apply. It does not carry spatial effects or grain.
Hald CLUTA LUT saved as a PNG image.
OCIOOpenColorIO, a colour management standard in film and graphics software.
DesqueezeStretching the image from an anamorphic lens to its correct proportions.
