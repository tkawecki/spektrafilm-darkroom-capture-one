Grain

The grain of colour film is clouds of dye in three layers, not noise. It has colour, and it depends on the density of the negative and on the frame size. Spektrafilm has several grain models; new looks start without grain.

Pitfalls

The panel greys out the controls that the chosen model does not use. With Synthesis, Grain amount, Grain saturation, the RMS granularity section and Particle grain are greyed out; Final blur (µm), Seed, Keep average colour under grain and Film format still work. With the other models, the Grain synthesis section is greyed out. The values stay saved.

Keep average colour under grain

Spektrafilm optics › Spektrafilm grain

New look
on

Grain drawn on pixels shifts the average density and, through the paper, the average colour. Without correction, the image with grain is about 3–8% darker and cooler at 24 MP, and the more so the higher the resolution. With this option on, the local average is brought back to that of the image without grain. The preview and the export then have the same colour.

When to turn it off

Only for comparisons with the original Spektrafilm.

Film format

Spektrafilm optics › Spektrafilm grain

Options
8 mm, Super 8, 16 mm, Super 16, 35 mm, Super 35, 65 mm, IMAX 70
Default
35 mm

The physical size of the frame. A smaller frame means greater enlargement, so larger grain and wider halation and scatter. It also scales the range of the DIR couplers and the scanner sharpness, so it changes the look even without grain.

Format and your camera

This setting describes the film frame you simulate, not the camera sensor. The whole photo is treated as the whole frame of the chosen format. For photos from full-frame, APS-C or medium-format cameras, leave 35 mm if you want the look of 35 mm film. Long side of the frame in the model: 8 mm 4.8 mm, Super 8 5.8 mm, 16 mm 10.3 mm, Super 16 12.5 mm, Super 35 24.9 mm, 35 mm 35 mm, 65 mm 52.5 mm, IMAX 70 70.4 mm. A crop in C1 cuts out part of this frame, like cutting out part of a negative: the grain stays tied to the whole photo, so after a strong crop and enlargement to the same size it looks larger. For even larger grain, use a smaller format or Enlarger scale.

When to use

The 65 mm format (large cinema frame) for smooth, fine grain; 16 mm or Super 8 for strong, cinematic grain.

Grain synthesis

Experimental feature

Grain synthesis can change brightness and colour, not only texture. It works up to 32 MP: with a larger preview or export the panel shows an error, and you have to lower the resolution or change the model. At the default settings it gives much stronger grain than Production, and stronger still with Blur grain as light off. For everyday work, choose Production.

Pitfall: graphics card protection

Settings whose work per pixel would exceed the GPU driver's time limit (many samples with a large radius spread, a high quantile or large radius scales) are rejected with a message instead of resetting the graphics card driver.

Capture One Grain

The separate Capture One Grain (applied to the C1 variant) section sets Capture One's own grain, not Spektrafilm's. The sliders change nothing until you click Apply grain. The panel then saves the C1 grain in the variant, and the grain acts before the film simulation, that is, like scene noise.

Apply grain, Restore grain

Capture One Grain (applied to the C1 variant)

Apply grain saves the C1 grain in the current variant; you can undo it with ordinary Ctrl+Z in C1. Restore grain brings back the grain the variant had before the first of a run of Apply grain clicks in this C1 session. The panel remembers it only until Capture One is closed.

When to use

Rarely. Spektrafilm grain (Production) is more natural, because it forms in the film and passes through the paper. C1 grain can be useful when you want “scene” noise that the film will then soften.

Grain for screen and for print

These are starting points from practice, not measurements. Judge the grain strength on your own print or screen.

  • Screen, Instagram, small images: Production, Grain amount 1–1.5. Downsizing weakens the grain anyway.
  • Prints 30×45 cm and larger: Production, amount 0.8–1, judged in the 100% preview (Viewer resolution = Auto or Native resolution).
  • Strong, cinematic grain: Film format 16 mm or Super 16 and Production.
