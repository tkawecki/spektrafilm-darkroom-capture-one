Working tools

Spektrafilm settings are saved separately for each variant, outside the Capture One settings. That is why the panel has its own undo, versions, presets, copying and comparisons.

Where you see the effect

Spektrafilm draws its image only in the main Capture One Viewer, laid over the selected photo. The rest of the program still shows the C1 development.

Using the sliders and the panel

  • Back to the default value: double-click the slider name or the slider itself.
  • Exact value: click the number field on the right, type the value and confirm. This way you can set values the slider cannot hit, e.g. Filter strength 0.3.
  • Cut-off names: since the panel build of 21 November 2026 (16:25), the slider name column fits the longest name, as long as the slider keeps at least half the width. With a panel 380 px wide or more, all names are shown in full; in a narrower panel the longest ones are shortened (“Scanner MTF50 (lp/…”). In that case widen the tool column or undock the panel.
  • Floating window: the three-dot menu in the panel header has the Float Tool command. The panel then opens in a separate window that you can widen and move to a second monitor.
  • Sections: click a header to collapse or expand a section. Nested sections, such as Scanning or Spektrafilm grain, are inside Spektrafilm optics.
  • Greyed-out controls do not work in the current mode or are waiting for another option. Their saved values stay and come back when the control is active again.

Undo

Undo, Redo

Under “Enable Spektrafilm”

Undo and redo Spektrafilm changes on the current photo. The shortcuts Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Y and Ctrl+Shift+Z work when the panel has focus, that is, after you click in the panel. After you click on the photo, Ctrl+Z goes back to Capture One.

  • Each photo has its own history of up to 200 steps, kept until you close the catalog or Capture One.
  • Changes made faster than 0.8 s apart, such as one continuous slider drag, are one step. A pause while dragging starts a new step.
  • Defaults, loading a preset, restoring a version, “Use recommended input” and “Apply copied look” are separate steps.
  • Undo restores the whole look: films, paper, optics, the on/off switch and the C1 grain sliders. It does not touch C1 grain already saved in the variant; ordinary Ctrl+Z in C1 undoes that.
Pitfalls

Ordinary Ctrl+Z in C1 does not undo Spektrafilm. If you have just selected another photo, the first Undo only tells you so, so that it does not change a photo you have not seen yet.

Look versions

Save look version…, Restore look version…

Look versions and copying (collapsible section under Defaults)

A version is a named record of the whole look of the current photo, kept between sessions. Save look version… asks for a name. Restore look version… shows a list of versions with dates and, before restoring, saves the current look as a version “Before restoring …”, so nothing is lost.

The panel saves a version by itself before actions that replace the whole look: Defaults (“Before defaults”), Load preset… (“Before loading preset …”), Use recommended input and Apply copied look to selected.

When to use

Before an experiment that may not work out. To compare several looks on one photo when you do not want clones in C1.

Defaults

Under the Viewer settings

Sets a new, default look: Portra 400 on Portra Endura, neutral ProPhoto input, automatic metering, no grain and no effects. The previous look is saved as the version “Before defaults”, and you can also undo it with Undo; the panel does not show a message about this. The Viewer settings do not change. Under the button there is the Automatic film exposure row with the Metering list (chapter 5), and below it the collapsible Look versions and copying section.

Copying and presets

Copy look, Apply copied look to selected

Look versions and copying (collapsible section under Defaults)

  1. On the photo with the finished look, click Copy look.
  2. Select one or more photos in C1.
  3. Click Apply copied look to selected. Each photo first gets a version “Before applying copied look”.
Pitfalls

A variant clone made in C1 does not inherit the Spektrafilm look: the look belongs to the variant, so a new clone starts from the default settings. Copy the look with Copy look. Checked in a test: after changing the film and Film exposure, the clone of the variant showed Defaults in the panel (Portra 400, 0 EV), while the original kept its look.

Ordinary Copy/Apply Adjustments in C1 does not carry the Spektrafilm settings. With automatic metering on, each photo is metered separately, so Film exposure works as an offset and brightness usually stays consistent across a series.

Save preset…, Load preset…

Presets and LUT export

A preset is a .spektra.json file with the whole look: film, paper, all sliders and optics. Use it to build your own library of looks and to move them between C1 catalogs. Load preset… first saves the current look as a version. If the version cannot be saved, the look stays unchanged.

Pitfalls

Looks are tied to the path of the catalog or session. After you move or rename the catalog, or open it on another computer, the panel will not find them. Before moving, save important looks as presets.

A preset with a film or paper that the panel does not have is rejected with a message. Looks saved with an earlier second processing engine, since removed, are no longer supported.

Comparisons and preview

Show source

Under the Look strength slider

Shows in the Viewer the C1 image prepared for the simulation (after Input preparation), without film. Useful for a quick before and after. The setting lasts until the end of the session and is not part of the look.

Pin reference, Compare reference…, Clear reference

Under the Look strength slider

Pin reference stores the current Viewer frame, which can also come from another photo. Compare reference… opens a comparison window with the modes Side by side, Vertical wipe, Reference only and Current only; the window follows the current Viewer. Clear reference removes the stored frame.

When to use

To compare two papers or two contrast values, or to match the look of a photo to another photo in the series.

Scopes…

Under the Look strength slider

Opens a window with an RGB histogram, a waveform and a vectorscope for the current Spektrafilm frame, in sRGB values 0–255. Options: Luma waveform (brightness waveform) and Log histogram (logarithmic scale). The pixel counts at 0 and 255 describe this 8-bit frame, not clipping in the RAW file.

Exposure strips…

Presets and LUT export

A test strip as in the darkroom: it renders 3 or 5 versions of the photo with different Film exposure, in steps of 1/3, 1/2 or 1 EV, as full frames or as a test strip. You carry the chosen exposure over to the look with the Apply selected exposure button.

When to use

At the start of work on a difficult photo, when you do not know which way to go with the exposure.

Save preview PNG…

Under the Look strength slider

Saves the current Viewer frame as a PNG in sRGB, at preview resolution. Use it to share a preview quickly; for the real files, use export. The panel will not let you overwrite the original photo.

Viewer

Viewer resolution

Under the preview buttons

Options
Auto (follows Viewer zoom), Native resolution, 4096, 3072, 2560, 2048, 1600, 1200, 900, 720 px
Default
Auto

The resolution of the full frame in the Viewer. The setting is shared by all photos, is not part of the look and does not affect export.

Auto (follows Viewer zoom) computes the frame at the size the photo takes up on screen (1600, 2048 or 2560 px), so after a settings change the finished image appears after about 0.1 s instead of about 1 s. When you zoom the Viewer in beyond that size, the panel adds full resolution after a moment. When less than half of the photo is visible (for example at 100% zoom), it renders only the visible part with a margin at full resolution, over the frame at screen size, which is faster than the whole photo; panning beyond the margin renders the new part as soon as the Viewer rests. The pixels are the same as in a full-resolution frame. When you zoom out, nothing is recomputed; the next change goes back to screen size. Capture One develops the photo once, as with Native.

When to use

Auto for everyday work: fast when you view the whole photo, full resolution when you zoom in. Native resolution when you want the full frame always ready, for example when you often jump between the whole-photo view and 100%. A fixed 2048–3072 px when you never need full resolution. With Grain synthesis and photos above 32 MP, choose a smaller fixed resolution; in Auto the preview works when you view the whole photo, but after zooming in to full resolution the panel shows a limit error.

Pitfalls

In Auto, judge grain and sharpness after zooming in: at screen size the grain is averaged. Save preview PNG… saves the frame under the zoomed-in part, so in Auto usually at screen size.

Drag preview resolution

Under the preview buttons

Options
Off, 720, 1200, 1600, 2048, 2560 px
Default
1600 px

A temporary, faster frame while you drag sliders with a large preview. After you release the slider, or after a single change, the panel first shows a frame at screen resolution and then the full one. Off turns the temporary frames off: the preview refreshes only when the change is finished. The setting is shared by all photos.

Monitor and preview colour

The Spektrafilm preview is computed in sRGB and then converted to your monitor profile from the Windows settings, just as Capture One does. On a wide-gamut monitor you therefore see correct sRGB colours, not oversaturated ones.

  • The preview does not show colours outside sRGB, even when you export to a wider colour space (chapter 11).
  • When HDR mode or automatic colour management (Advanced Color) is on in Windows, the panel hands colour over to the system and shows a notice about it. This mode has not been compared by measurement with the ICC profile mode.
  • The match between the preview and the screen has not yet been confirmed by measurement with a colorimeter. For colour-critical judgement, compare the export in a program you trust.
  • Save preview PNG… saves the preview in sRGB with an embedded sRGB profile.

Performance

Approximate timings on an RTX 4090 card for a 50 MP photo: about 20 frames per second while you drag a slider, and a finished frame at screen resolution about 0.1 s after a change. With Viewer resolution = Auto this is already the final frame; with Native resolution, or after zooming in, the full frame follows after about 1 s. After you switch photos, the first image with film appears after about 0.2–0.3 s (it is computed from the ready Capture One preview, so for a moment it is less sharp), and the full frame after about 2–3 s. After a change of settings in Capture One itself (exposure, white balance), the image with film refreshes after about 0.2–0.3 s, and while you drag a Capture One slider, a dozen or so times per second; the finished frame arrives about 1.3–2.3 s after the slider stops. After C1 starts, the full frame appears after about 4 s.

Spatial effects: halation, diffusion, a wide coupler rangeSet the films, exposure and contrast first, and add the optics at the end.

Export quality does not depend on these settings: export always computes full quality.
