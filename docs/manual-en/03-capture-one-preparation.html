Preparation in Capture One

Spektrafilm receives the image that Capture One has developed and treats it as scene light falling on the film. The more neutral and true to the scene this image is, the better the simulation works.

Working variant

Spektrafilm settings are saved separately for each variant of a photo. It is easiest to work on a clone: the original variant keeps your existing edits, and the clone gets the film look. Spektrafilm is, however, on by default for every variant without a saved look, so on the original clear Enable Spektrafilm if it should stay without film (also in export). You can have several clones with different film–paper pairs. A new clone starts from the Spektrafilm default settings; you carry a look over to it with Copy look (chapter 10). Compare clones one at a time or with the Compare reference… window, because the Spektrafilm preview is paused when the Viewer shows several photos at once.

Tip

You do not need to reset photos you have already edited. A clone with neutral look settings on the clone, as described below, is enough.

What to keep in Capture One

These adjustments prepare the scene and do not compete with the film:

  • White balance. Set it neutral: grey and white surfaces should have no colour cast. The simulation assumes a correctly balanced image. You add a warm or cool mood later, on purpose, with white balance or enlarger filtration (chapter 6).
  • Crop, straightening, perspective and lens correction. Geometry has no effect on the simulation.
  • Noise reduction and retouching. Sensor noise and film grain are different things. Too much noise mixes with the grain. Spektrafilm receives an image that is already denoised: ordinary Noise Reduction and Enhanced Denoise work before the film simulation, and the film and paper raise contrast, so leftover noise becomes more visible than in C1. When you turn on Enhanced Denoise, C1 spends a few seconds computing a separate file in the background. Until then the panel shows the photo with ordinary noise reduction, and once the file is ready it renders the photo again by itself (panel version of 21 November 2026 onwards; on a test photo this clearly reduced the noise in dark areas).
  • Local brightness and colour adjustments. They work before the film, that is, like brightening or darkening parts of the scene. This is a good place to lift a face out of shadow.

What to reduce or turn off

These adjustments build a look of their own. On the film they would stack on top of the film look:

  • Curves, Levels, Contrast, Brightness. Contrast and tonality come from the film–paper pair.
  • Color grading, Color Editor, saturation, Vibrance. Colour comes from the film–paper pair and the enlarger filtration.
  • Styles, look presets and LUTs. Turn them off on the working variant.
  • Strong High Dynamic Range (Highlight/Shadow). A little helps in extreme scenes, but strong settings flatten the image even before the film.
  • Film Grain in C1. Grain is better added in Spektrafilm. The panel also has a section that sets C1 grain, if you prefer it (chapter 8).
Pitfall

Double contrast is the most common mistake. The C1 curve plus the film and paper curves give a harsh, blown-out photo, and different films start to look alike.

Input to the simulation

Four settings decide in what form the image from C1 reaches the film. New looks have them set correctly; check older ones.

Input preparation

Spektrafilm optics › Color management

Options
Neutral for film, Capture One development
New look
Neutral for film

Decides how C1 prepares the image for Spektrafilm. It works on a copy of the photo’s settings, only for the Spektrafilm preview and export. Your saved C1 settings do not change.

Neutral for film

C1 uses the Linear Response curve that matches the camera instead of the usual development curve. It skips sharpening, Clarity and Structure, also on layers, and output sharpening. The other adjustments work: exposure, white balance, crop, lens and local adjustments.

Side effect: the print is softer than the image in C1, because there is no digital sharpening. You restore sharpness with scanner sharpening, micro contrast or the film edge effect (chapter 9, Sharpness).

Capture One development

Spektrafilm receives the image exactly as you see it in C1, with its curve. The contrast doubles, and different films look more alike. This option is mainly for older looks that were made before neutral input was introduced.

When to use

Always Neutral for film, unless you deliberately want a stronger image based on the C1 look.

Pitfalls

If C1 has no Linear Response curve for your camera, the panel shows an error and offers to switch to Capture One development. The panel does not substitute another camera’s curve. Linear Response still has its own gentle highlight shaping, so it is not a scientific, fully linear image of the scene.

Input color space

Spektrafilm optics › Color management

Options
sRGB, Adobe RGB (1998), ProPhoto RGB, Linear Rec.2020
New look
ProPhoto RGB

The colour space in which C1 hands the image to the simulation. A wide space keeps the saturated colours of the scene, for example the blue of the sea, the green of plants or the red of roof tiles. The film compresses them in its own way.

When to use

ProPhoto RGB for everyday work. sRGB clips saturated colours before they reach the film and remains only for older looks. Linear Rec.2020 is a wide space without a gamma curve, useful for technical comparisons.

Use recommended input

Button below the Look strength slider, visible only when the input is not the recommended one

Sets Neutral for film and ProPhoto RGB for the current look. It first saves the existing look as the version “Before recommended input”, so you can always go back to it.

Pitfalls

After the switch, brightness and contrast change. Adjust Film exposure.

Renderer transport encoding (technical)

Spektrafilm optics › Color management

Only determines how pixels travel from the renderer to the panel. The panel decodes them back before display and export, so the look does not change. Leave it at sRGB.

JPEG and TIFF photos

Neutral input needs a Linear Response curve for the camera, and Capture One provides it for RAW files from supported cameras. For JPEGs, TIFFs and scans there is usually no such curve, and the panel then shows the message “A matching Capture One Linear Response curve is unavailable…”.

  • For such a photo, set Input preparation = Capture One development.
  • The image then already has the tone curve of the camera or the software, so the contrast doubles. Start with a lower Print gamma (0.7–0.85) and a negative Print highlight shape.
  • Sharpening saved in the JPEG stays in the image. Do not add scanner sharpening on top.
  • Highlights clipped in the JPEG are lost; film cannot bring them back.

According to users on the Spektrafilm forum, the simulation works on such files but needs more manual correction than on RAW files.

Exposure in C1 and in Spektrafilm

With Automatic film exposure on, the panel first meters the image from C1 and sets the film exposure so that the midtones land in the middle of the curve. A change of exposure in C1 is therefore largely evened out by the metering. Set brightness with the Film exposure and Print exposure sliders.

Exposure in C1 matters mainly when you turn off automatic metering. Darkening the photo in C1 does not recover highlights: with Linear Response, C1 already shows the full range of the RAW (measured in the project on 17 November 2026), and the C1 exposure slider also changes colour along the way. Metering is described in chapter 5.

Pitfall

C1 clips highlights at the sensor’s white level. What is not in the RAW, film cannot bring back. Bright highlights in the scene, needed for halation, can be boosted with the Highlight boost slider (chapter 7).

Checklist before turning on Spektrafilm

  1. Variant clone made.
  2. White balance and exposure correct for the scene.
  3. Crop and geometry done.
  4. Curves, color grading, styles and LUTs neutral on the clone.
  5. In the panel, Input preparation = Neutral for film, Input color space = ProPhoto RGB.
